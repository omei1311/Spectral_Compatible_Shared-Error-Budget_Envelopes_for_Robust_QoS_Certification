[Title    ] Sharp Robust QoS Certification from Six-Scalar Spectral Summaries
[         ] Fixed beamforming and RIS configurations under a partial-information model
[         ] Version 6.5 • English revision • 8 October 2026
[Heading1 ] Abstract
[         ] Certifying that a fixed beamforming and reconfigurable intelligent
[         ] surface (RIS) configuration satisfies quality-of-service constraints
[         ] under a norm-bounded channel error is, given the complete channel and
[         ] interference geometry, a trust-region problem that exact methods already
[         ] solve; this paper therefore does not address full-information
[         ] optimization. Instead it asks a partial-information question: when the
[         ] certification interface retains only six scalar summaries — the
[         ] desired-stream power, the nominal desired and interference amplitudes,
[         ] and three gains of the interference operator — what is the strongest
[         ] worst-case QoS guarantee that holds uniformly over every instance
[         ] consistent with those summaries? The three gains originate from one
[         ] operator and obey spectral compatibility that a separate-gain envelope
[         ] ignores. Exploiting this compatibility yields a continuous three-branch
[         ] directional interference extremum in closed form. The induced QoS
[         ] certificate is safe, dominates the separate-gain envelope pointwise
[         ] under the same six scalars, and — the main result — equals the infimum
[         ] of the true worst-case margin over the entire nondegenerate information
[         ] class, so no uniformly better certificate exists at this information
[         ] budget. At the degenerate boundary where the desired direction attains
[         ] the total norm, a distinct phase-coupled envelope is derived and
[         ] attained explicitly. Experiments verify the analytical formulas,
[         ] exhibit strict but modest and batch-dependent empirical gains over the
[         ] separate-gain envelope, and quantify how norm-restricted information
[         ] classes can certify strictly more.
[         ] Keywords: robust QoS certification; partial information; information class; spectral compatibility; RIS; fixed configuration.
[Heading1 ] 1 Introduction
[         ] After beamforming vectors and discrete RIS phases have been fixed for a candidate configuration, deployment, reuse, and screening still require verifying that every user's QoS constraint survives channel-state uncertainty. With the complete channel and interference geometry available, this verification is a ball-constrained quadratic problem that trust-region methods and the S-procedure solve exactly [2],[3],[4]; robust transmission design with imperfect CSI, including bounded-error RIS settings, is correspondingly mature [1],[4],[9]. The existence of exact full-information solvers is therefore not the issue this paper addresses.
[         ] In candidate screening over many configurations, when configuration descriptors are cached or exchanged across network components, or when a stored candidate is evaluated at several uncertainty radii, a compact fixed-size summary can be preferable to carrying the full quadratic geometry. This paper fixes the information budget deliberately: six scalar summaries — the desired-stream power, the nominal desired and interference amplitudes, and three gains of the interference operator. The research question is then exact: what is the strongest worst-case QoS guarantee that can be certified uniformly over all full channel/interference instances consistent with exactly these summaries? The paper does not study full-information robust beamforming optimization, optimal RIS phase design, the optimal compression dimension, or whether six scalars are the best descriptor; it studies what the spectral compatibility induced by the common operator origin of the three gains contributes to the uniform certificate at this fixed budget. Envelopes that use the three gains separately ignore the fact that all three originate from one operator and therefore cannot attain their individual worst cases simultaneously.
[         ] We answer the question in three steps. First, we solve the directional interference extremum under the joint spectral constraints: given the desired-direction gain α, the orthogonal-subspace gain β, and the total spectral norm β_0, we evaluate the largest interference output that a parallel error amplitude x and an orthogonal budget y can jointly produce. The extremum is a continuous three-branch closed form proved directly from a two-by-two determinant argument, in the same spirit as classical contraction-completion and Buzano-type refined Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities [5],[6],[7]. Second, we embed the extremum into the worst-case QoS margin, obtaining a shared-error-budget lower envelope. Third, we ask whether any safer certificate using the same six scalars can be strictly better, and we answer in the negative away from a degenerate spectral boundary.
[         ] The envelope is safe and dominates the separate-gain comparison envelope pointwise. Over the nondegenerate information class (β_0>α) the envelope equals the infimum of the true worst-case margin over all instances with the same six summaries, and an explicit construction sequence approaches this infimum — a sharpness statement related to the information-based viewpoint of optimal recovery [8]. At the degenerate boundary β_0=α, the equality β_0=α is itself one of the summaries; it forces the parallel and orthogonal interference outputs to be orthogonal, so the desired signal and the parallel interference share one phase within the boundary class, and the optimal envelope of that class is different and is attained by an explicit diagonal construction. The two envelopes can differ sharply for the same boundary summaries (0.59 versus −0.21 in a constructed example); the discontinuity is a property of the information classes, not of any physical channel.
[         ] The numerical study has five roles: verifying the analytical implementation to machine precision; exhibiting constructed instances where the new envelope certifies feasibility that the separate-gain envelope cannot; validating on preregistered, previously unused seeds, including a heterogeneous-link pool; showing that the six-scalar certificate and a quadratic bound retaining one additional scalar are complementary (no universal dominance, safe to combine by maximization); and cross-validating the certification statements of the validation batch with an independent S-procedure SDP formulation. The experiments support implementation correctness and complementary use; they do not prove the theorems, and the empirical acceptance gain over the separate-gain envelope is positive in the sensitivity, validation and heterogeneous-link batches, zero in a saturated easy batch, and modest wherever positive.
[         ] This work makes three contributions. First, a closed-form
[         ] spectral-compatible directional extremum: from the joint constraints
[         ] imposed by the desired-direction gain, the orthogonal-subspace gain
[         ] and the total spectral norm of one interference operator, an explicit
[         ] continuous three-branch maximum of the interference output under a
[         ] shared error budget is derived and attained exactly over the
[         ] three-spectral operator class. Second, information-class optimality:
[         ] the induced shared-budget QoS envelope is safe, dominates the envelope
[         ] that uses the three gains separately under the same six scalars, and
[         ] equals the information-class infimum away from the degenerate
[         ] boundary, with a separately derived and explicitly attained
[         ] phase-coupled envelope at that boundary. Third, numerical verification
[         ] of the analytical claims — formula correctness, construction
[         ] attainment, boundary behaviour — together with an honest evaluation of
[         ] the conservatism and complementarity of the six-scalar interface,
[         ] including bounded-nominal-norm experiments that quantify what
[         ] additional physical information would buy.

[Heading2 ] 2.3 Six-scalar information model and certification hierarchy
[         ] The six retained summaries define an information map, not a physical channel model. Let ℐ𝒳 denote the six-scalar summary of a full configuration instance 𝒳=b,ĥ,C at a user, and let
[Equation ] 𝒞s=𝒳:ℐ𝒳=s(2c)
[         ] be the class of all instances indistinguishable to a certificate that observes only s. Throughout, the error radius r, the SINR level γ, the noise power σ2 and the dimensions n,m are fixed external parameters of the certification problem; they are not part of the summary s and are never varied inside an information class. A six-scalar certificate is any function Bs of the retained summaries that must remain valid across the entire class, Bs≤q*𝒳 for all 𝒳∈𝒞s, and the strongest possible uniform certificate under this information budget is therefore
[Equation ] B*s=inf𝒳∈𝒞s q*𝒳(2d)
[         ] Three levels should be kept apart: the full physical instance 𝒳; the information map ℐ that compresses it to s; and the information class 𝒞s. The quantity B*s is not the true robust margin of any particular physical instance; it is the best uniform certificate that any method observing only the retained summaries can guarantee. Information-class sharpness is therefore a statement of information sufficiency, not instance-wise exactness: QG may be conservative for a particular fixed operator while remaining optimal uniformly over the entire information class. Sections 3–5 characterize this quantity from the retained spectral information: Section 3 solves the directional extremum forced by the operator origin of the summaries; Section 4 proves that the resulting envelope equals B*s on the nondegenerate class and characterizes the separate optimum of the boundary class β0=α.
[         ] The information map defines a branched family of certification interfaces rather than a single linear ladder. With the same six scalars one may use the three gains separately (the separate-gain comparison envelope of this paper) or jointly through spectral compatibility (the envelope of this paper). Two distinct one-scalar augmentations are of interest: adjoining κ=∥A ĥ∥, the norm of the gradient of the quadratic objective, yields the quadratic-form bound Lquad=cq−2rκ−γβ02r2 of Section 7; adjoining H=∥ĥ∥ restricts the class itself, for which we give a necessary compatibility condition but no complete envelope. Finally, the full quadratic data support cheap bounds and exact trust-region or S-procedure solvers. The two augmentations are different information branches and are not merged into a single seven-scalar class. Adding information can only leave the strongest uniform certificate B* unchanged or tighter; it does not imply that any particular computable bound tightens, and indeed QG and Lquad exhibit no universal dominance relation in either direction. The interfaces also differ in cost structure: the six scalars and κ are built once from matrix geometry; their storage and transfer are then fixed-size; subsequent bound evaluation is scalar work; exact fallback, in contrast, requires the full quadratic data.
[         ] Why not retain one more scalar? Nothing prevents it: this paper answers the optimality question for exactly one fixed interface, and richer summaries — κ, H, or others — are legitimate alternatives whose complete envelopes are open. The six-scalar interface is studied because, together with the nominal amplitudes required by the QoS envelope, it retains the three spectral quantities of one operator, so that joint spectral compatibility and the strongest uniform guarantee can be analyzed in closed form at a fixed summary size. We make no claim that six scalars are the best compression, that only this interface can be fixed-size, or any measured storage or communication saving.
[         ] Such a fixed-size interface can arise when candidate configurations are screened repeatedly, when configuration descriptors are cached or exchanged across network components, or when one wishes to evaluate a stored candidate at multiple uncertainty radii without retaining the complete quadratic geometry. These scenarios motivate the abstraction but are not assumptions required by the theory.
[Heading2 ] 2.4 Physical interpretation of the retained summaries
[         ] The retained quantities separate the desired direction from its orthogonal complement and summarize how one interference operator acts on these two subspaces. In wireless terms: p is the desired-stream transmit power; a is the nominal desired-signal amplitude along the desired beam; I0 is the nominal interference amplitude, whose square contributes to the interference power. The three spectral quantities describe how the interference operator maps channel error to interference: α is its response to error parallel to the desired beam; β is the largest amplification any error component orthogonal to the beam can suffer; β0 is the largest amplification any error at all can suffer.
[         ] The three spectral quantities should not be viewed as independent uncertainty parameters. They are coupled observations of the same interference operator. Spectral compatibility is therefore a structural consistency condition: a large response in one direction restricts what the same operator can realize in orthogonal directions.
[         ] A bound that uses these gains separately may combine individually valid worst cases that cannot be realized simultaneously by one operator and one shared error vector. The proposed directional extremum removes exactly this incompatibility while retaining the same six scalar summaries. Its improvement over the separate-gain envelope therefore comes from using more of the structure already contained in the retained information, rather than from introducing additional channel information.
[Heading2 ] 2.5 Related work
[         ] Robust transmission under imperfect CSI splits along the information available. Full-geometry worst-case design under norm-bounded errors is classical: S-procedure and semidefinite reformulations deliver exact or conservative constraints for MISO downlink, relay, secure, and RIS-aided systems, with [1] an early IRS-aided framework, [4] a current multi-functional RIS design under bounded cascaded CSI error, and [9] a representative worst-case SINR treatment that decomposes the steering-vector error into parallel and orthogonal components. Exact solution of the ball-constrained quadratic rests on the trust-region and S-lemma literature [2],[3]. Decomposing an error into parallel and orthogonal components and bounding the two effects separately is a recurring device in this line; the comparison envelope of the present paper — the shared-budget bound minβ0r,αx+βy assembled from the two standard norm constraints — is our baseline in that spirit; we have not found this exact envelope stated as a method in the literature and do not attribute it to any specific paper. A second line compresses information probabilistically, using distributional summaries with conservative approximations. Our question differs from both: the uncertainty is deterministic and bounded, and the compressed object is a fixed set of spectral summaries of one operator.

[         ] The mathematics we need is classical but dispersed, and we use it at the formula level detailed in Appendix A. The cross-term bound in our extremum is a Buzano-type refinement of Cauchy–Schwarz [5]; the two-by-two positive-semidefinite completion behind our extremum is proved directly by a determinant computation, and the chordal completion theorem [6] and the contraction-completion framework of Parrott [7] place it in a wider theory without our result being a direct substitution of either; and the strongest-uniform-certificate quantity B*s is related to the information-based viewpoint of optimal recovery [8] — we do not claim equivalence with a radius of information in the sense of a recovery-error bound. To the best of our knowledge, within the searches reported in Appendix A, we have not found an existing result that evaluates the directional extremum in closed form under exactly the three retained spectral summaries and a shared error budget, nor an existing treatment of the β_0=α boundary's phase-coupled envelope; we do not claim these ingredients individually as new.

[         ] For reference evaluation define A=bbH−γCCH, g=Aĥ and cq=ĥHAĥ−γσ2=pa2−γI02−γσ2. The margin is cq+eHAe+2ReeHg. The common engineering threshold is

[         ] The theoretical envelope depends only on six summaries; this experimental threshold and the reference validity checks additionally use full quadratic data. [         ] The reference solves the Hermitian trust region problem by eigenvalue decomposition and a multiplier search, handling PSD interior solutions, singular cases and the indefinite hard case. [Heading2 ] 6.3 S procedure SDP baseline
[         ] As a full information baseline independent of our eigendecomposition reference, we adapt the standard single sphere S procedure LMI to the fixed candidate certification problem: for each user, maximize t subject to λ≥0 and
[Equation ] A+λIggHcq−t−λr2≽0,(31)
[         ] whose optimum equals q*r by the S lemma; the LMI is solved on the real embedding of the Hermitian data with an interior point solver [2],[3],[4]. [         ] The SDP provides a lower bound, but a negative lower bound proves nothing about infeasibility, and a solver status of optimal is not a certificate. Our decision rule is two sided. The lower bound L is the residual corrected dual feasible value: the shift t,λ→t−η1+r2,λ+η adds blockdiagηI,η to the LMI, so Weyl's inequality restores positive semidefiniteness up to eigensolver rounding, with an empirical rounding allowance and a numerical recheck; this is an implementation detail, not a formal error bound. Infeasibility is certified only by an upper bound: from the corrected multiplier we recover the Lagrangian minimizer A+λ'Ie=−g, project it onto the ball if ∥e∥>r, and re evaluate the true objective U=qew. Since ew is ball feasible, U is a valid upper bound of q*, and U<−τ certifies infeasibility; no optimality of ew is claimed. All remaining cases return unresolved and are never treated as rejections.

[         ] Staged screening pipelines complete the same final decision task: bounds are evaluated only on users not yet certified, a failed bound is never a rejection, the tolerance-stopped exact reference ends a candidate at the first clearly infeasible user, and each user's factorization is cached. With one warm up and three interleaved repetitions, the ordering quadratic bound, then envelope, then reference — selected by a fixed rule on the exploratory cohort and frozen, with code hashes and unused seeds recorded before any new computation — attains a median paired speedup of 2.12 over the pure tolerance-stopped reference (25 of 30 pairs) on the exploratory grid and 2.01 (11 of 15) on five fresh confirmation seeds (221–225), reducing total reference calls by 75% and 72% respectively. The quadratic bound alone is the fastest single bound pipeline in the tested implementation; the envelope stage contributes the additional certified units and a further 7% reduction in reference calls, at some increase in pipeline time relative to the quadratic-only stage. Adding the envelope raises coverage; it does not, by itself, reduce wall-clock time. All pipelines produce identical final decisions in every run. An adaptive interval refinement (64→256→1024→4096 cells, stopping when a rounding guarded feasible point value shows the threshold cannot be crossed) reproduces fixed 4096 cell acceptance exactly with 3.9× fewer interval cells but is slower in wall clock at this scale and is not used.
[         ] A focused interface study (seeds 401–402, preregistered configuration) implements strict query-side clients — the six-scalar client receives only p,a,I0,α,β,β0 plus the external radius and parameters, the seven-scalar client adds κ, and a compact exact client receives only the eigendecomposed spectrum λ,w,c of A,g — and measures actual batch totals for 1, 10 and 100 repeated radius queries (longer batches cycle the same deterministic grid). Numerical payloads are 48 and 56 float64 bytes for the summary clients, 144 bytes for the compact exact cache (λ, w, c plus scale, actual array nbytes), and about 1.7 KB for the full-geometry cache. Amortized per query, the seven-scalar quadratic certificate is the cheapest branch (well under 1 µs), the six-scalar envelope client costs about 0.9 µs, and the compact exact cache about 25 µs — so the strict seven-scalar client is faster than the six-scalar one, and the compact exact cache shrinks the storage gap between summaries and exact certificates to a factor of about three. Direct certification coverage over the 240 candidate–radius units is 119 for QG, 123 for Lquad and 124 for the joint rule (91.5%, 94.6% and 95.4% of the 130 reference-feasible units; the joint rule adds exactly one unit over Lquad in this pool). With summary screening and exact fallback, 217 compact-cache queries complete all 240 exact decisions with no unresolved units. The study shows no runtime or storage advantage of the six-scalar interface over the seven-scalar one; the envelope's practical contribution here is coverage supplementation.

[Heading2 ] 7.6 Effect of a bounded nominal-channel norm
[         ] Two numerical studies estimate how much the unrestricted sharp
[         ] envelope loses when the class is restricted by ∥ĥ∥≤Hmax. The "
[         ] "estimates are bracketed: QGs=B*s is a rigorous lower bound "
[         ] "for BH*s, and a multi-start feasible search over class "
[         ] "instances (random unitary conjugations fixing v, exact matching of "
[         ] "a and I0 on random orthogonal directions, exact trust-region "
[         ] "evaluation) provides an *upper* estimate; no global optimality of "
[         ] "the restricted search is claimed.
[         ] *Boundary-approach family (Section 5).* For the family with "
[         ] "δ∈0.1,0.03,0.01 and Hmax∈2,…,100, the upper "
[         ] "estimate stays near 0.59 — close to the boundary-class value — "
[         ] "whenever the divergent family member (norms 20.0, 66.7, "
[         ] "200.0) is excluded, and drops to QG≈−0.21 exactly when "
[         ] "Hmax admits it. The restricted gap upper estimate reaches "
[         ] "≈0.8: for these summaries, the unrestricted sharp value is driven "
[         ] "by large-norm geometry, and a norm bound that excludes it can "
[         ] "tighten the class optimum back toward the boundary value.
[         ] *Pool instances (validation seeds 211–213, three users each, "
[         ] "Hmax=c∥ĥ∥, c∈1,…,5, 120 starts).* The search found no "
[         ] "class member below the actual instance even at c=5, so the gap "
[         ] "upper estimate equals the actual-instance envelope looseness "
[         ] "(median 0.14, 90th percentile 0.33, maximum 0.54 of 45 "
[         ] "cases); none was near zero. Whether the restricted infimum for "
[         ] "these summaries genuinely exceeds QG remains unresolved — the "
[         ] "upper estimate is not tight — so the pool study neither proves nor "
[         ] "excludes a substantial restricted-class improvement.
[         ] Together, the two studies show that the unrestricted sharpness of "
[         ] "Theorem 2 is tight in a class-dependent way: norm restrictions can "
[         ] "certify strictly more for boundary-approaching summaries, while for "
[         ] "generic pool summaries the question is open. A complete "
[         ] "H-restricted envelope is left as future work.
[Heading2 ] 7.7 Timing and implementation sensitivity
[         ] Table 11 reports the same final decision task against the validated tolerance-stopped reference, which is the primary comparison. With the tolerance stop, the pure reference runs at 0.041 s per seed and threshold on the validation batch; among the two pipelines of Table 11, the new bound with tolerance-stopped fallback (0.032 s) is about 20% below it, while the same screening pipeline with the original 200 iteration fallback (0.075 s) is slower — part of the apparent saving in early measurements traced to multiplier search over iteration in the reference solver rather than to screening. The new pipeline is faster than the separate-gain pipeline in 6 of 15 validation pairs and 11–13 of 30 exploratory pairs across runs; no stable runtime advantage over the separate-gain envelope is observed. Table 10 reproduces the original 200 iteration protocol as a supplementary record. The results do not establish complexity advantages or superiority over an optimized solver. On the same batch, the generic SDP pipeline is 17.3 times slower than the tolerance-stopped reference as a ratio of medians (median per-pair slowdown 15.7, range 13.2–18.8; the reference is faster in 15 of 15 pairs), and the staged Lquad→QG→SDP pipeline is 6.5 times slower (median per-pair slowdown 7.2, range 1.5–12.4); these are single-round figures from the corrected SDP study, with all ratios defined as pipeline time divided by reference time. The SDP pipeline's value is cross validation, and the slowness of the generic conic path is not a statement about full-information methods.

[Heading1 ] 8 Interface to configuration lifecycle management
[         ] The certificate addresses whether a fixed candidate can be deployed at a prescribed uncertainty radius. Once a candidate is selected, a separate complete configuration reference can determine its robust radius for subsequent online retain or refresh management. Candidate generation, deployment costs and channel drift remain outside the present problem. A positive certificate at one radius is not a maximum radius certificate.
[Heading1 ] 9 Discussion
[         ] Four limitations bound the claims. (i) The six-scalar interface is
[         ] fixed by the problem formulation; the paper does not prove that six
[         ] scalars are Pareto-optimal among compressed representations, and the
[         ] interface study shows no runtime or storage advantage over a "
[         ] "seven-scalar branch. (ii) Information-class sharpness holds for the "
[         ] "unrestricted class; additional physical restrictions — including a "
[         ] "bounded nominal-channel norm — can only tighten the class optimum, "
[         ] "and the boundary-approach experiments show the tightening can be "
[         ] "large for some summaries. (iii) The empirical advantage over the "
[         ] "separate-gain envelope is strict but modest and dataset dependent "
[         ] "(zero in a saturated batch; +1 to +10 units elsewhere). (iv) The "
[         ] "floating-point implementation is numerically guarded but is not a "
[         ] "formally verified interval-arithmetic certificate. The paper's "
[         ] "retained significance is that it completely characterizes what can "
[         ] "and cannot be guaranteed from this particular information map: the "
[         ] "envelope, its sharpness, and the degenerate boundary are all "
[         ] "explicit, so any future richer-interface certificate can be "
[         ] "measured against a known optimum rather than an open gap.
[Heading1 ] 10 Conclusion
[         ] This paper studies robust QoS certification at a fixed information
[         ] budget of six scalar summaries. Because the three spectral gains
[         ] originate from one interference operator, their compatibility admits
[         ] an explicit three-branch directional extremum; lifting it through the
[         ] shared error budget yields a QoS certificate that is safe, pointwise
[         ] dominates the separate-gain envelope under the same six scalars, and
[         ] — the central result — equals the information-class infimum away from
[         ] the degenerate boundary, with an explicitly attained phase-coupled
[         ] envelope at that boundary. The class-optimal value is a statement of
[         ] information sufficiency, not instance-wise exactness, and norm-"
[         ] "restricted classes can certify strictly more, as the bounded-norm "
[         ] "experiments quantify. A natural next step is to characterize the "
[         ] "optimal certificate under richer or physically restricted "
[         ] "information classes, including classes that retain a bound on the "
[         ] "nominal-channel norm.
