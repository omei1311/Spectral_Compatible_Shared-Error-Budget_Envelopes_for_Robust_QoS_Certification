[Title    ] Sharp Robust QoS Certification from Six-Scalar Spectral Summaries
[         ] Fixed beamforming and RIS configurations under a partial-information model
[         ] Version 6.1 • English revision • 7 October 2026
[Heading1 ] Abstract
[         ] Certifying that a fixed beamforming and reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) configuration satisfies quality-of-service constraints under a norm-bounded channel-state error is, with full geometry, a trust region problem solvable exactly. This paper asks what can be guaranteed when the certification interface retains only six scalar summaries of the nominal channel and the interference operator: desired and nominal amplitudes, desired-direction gain, orthogonal-subspace gain, and total spectral norm. Because these gains originate from one operator, they obey spectral compatibility that a comparison envelope using the gains separately ignores. We solve the resulting directional interference extremum in closed form — a continuous three-branch envelope — and obtain a safe worst-case QoS bound that dominates the separate-gain envelope under the same information. For the nondegenerate information class we prove the bound equals the infimum of the true worst-case margin over all instances sharing the six summaries, and exhibit an approximating construction sequence; at the degenerate boundary where the desired direction attains the total norm, a distinct phase-coupled envelope is derived and attained explicitly. Numerical checks reproduce the analytical envelopes; on the validation batch two independent exact formulations agree, and the certification gain over the separate-gain envelope is strict but modest and batch-dependent; the certificate is complementary to a quadratic bound that retains one additional scalar.
[         ] Keywords: robust QoS certification; partial information; information class; spectral compatibility; RIS; fixed configuration.
[Heading1 ] 1 Introduction
[         ] After beamforming vectors and discrete RIS phases have been fixed for a candidate configuration, deployment, reuse, and screening still require verifying that every user's QoS constraint survives channel-state uncertainty. With the complete channel and interference geometry available, this verification is a ball-constrained quadratic problem that trust-region methods and the S-procedure solve exactly [2],[3],[4]; robust transmission design with imperfect CSI, including bounded-error RIS settings, is correspondingly mature [1],[4],[9]. The existence of exact full-information solvers is therefore not the issue this paper addresses.
[         ] In candidate screening over many configurations, when configuration descriptors are cached or exchanged across network components, or when a stored candidate is evaluated at several uncertainty radii, a compact fixed-size summary can be preferable to carrying the full quadratic geometry. If only six scalar summaries are retained — the desired-stream power, the nominal desired and interference amplitudes, and three gains of the interference operator — a precise question arises: what is the strongest worst-case QoS guarantee that is uniform over every instance consistent with these summaries? Envelopes that use the three gains separately ignore the fact that all three originate from one operator and therefore cannot attain their individual worst cases simultaneously.
[         ] We answer the question in three steps. First, we solve the directional interference extremum under the joint spectral constraints: given the desired-direction gain α, the orthogonal-subspace gain β, and the total spectral norm β_0, we evaluate the largest interference output that a parallel error amplitude x and an orthogonal budget y can jointly produce. The extremum is a continuous three-branch closed form proved directly from a two-by-two determinant argument, in the same spirit as classical contraction-completion and Buzano-type refined Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities [5],[6],[7]. Second, we embed the extremum into the worst-case QoS margin, obtaining a shared-error-budget lower envelope. Third, we ask whether any safer certificate using the same six scalars can be strictly better, and we answer in the negative away from a degenerate spectral boundary.
[         ] The envelope is safe and dominates the separate-gain comparison envelope pointwise. Over the nondegenerate information class (β_0>α) the envelope equals the infimum of the true worst-case margin over all instances with the same six summaries, and an explicit construction sequence approaches this infimum — a sharpness statement related to the information-based viewpoint of optimal recovery [8]. At the degenerate boundary β_0=α, the equality β_0=α is itself one of the summaries; it forces the parallel and orthogonal interference outputs to be orthogonal, so the desired signal and the parallel interference share one phase within the boundary class, and the optimal envelope of that class is different and is attained by an explicit diagonal construction. The two envelopes can differ sharply for the same boundary summaries (0.59 versus −0.21 in a constructed example); the discontinuity is a property of the information classes, not of any physical channel.
[         ] The numerical study has five roles: verifying the analytical implementation to machine precision; exhibiting constructed instances where the new envelope certifies feasibility that the separate-gain envelope cannot; validating on preregistered, previously unused seeds, including a heterogeneous-link pool; showing that the six-scalar certificate and a quadratic bound retaining one additional scalar are complementary (no universal dominance, safe to combine by maximization); and cross-validating the certification statements of the validation batch with an independent S-procedure SDP formulation. The experiments support implementation correctness and complementary use; they do not prove the theorems, and the empirical acceptance gain over the separate-gain envelope is positive in the sensitivity, validation and heterogeneous-link batches, zero in a saturated easy batch, and modest wherever positive.
[         ] This work makes three contributions. First, we derive the spectral-compatible directional envelope: from the joint constraints imposed by the desired-direction gain, the orthogonal-subspace gain, and the total spectral norm of one interference operator, we obtain an explicit continuous three-branch extremum for the worst interference output under a shared error budget. Second, we establish information-class optimality: the induced shared-budget QoS envelope is safe, dominates the envelope that uses the three gains separately, equals the information-class infimum away from the degenerate boundary, and is complemented by a separate, explicitly attained phase-coupled envelope at the boundary β_0=α. Third, we specify and validate a certification interface: exact trust-region and S-procedure references confirm the implementation on every validation problem, the envelope produces certifications the separate-gain envelope cannot, and, having no universal dominance relation with the one-additional-scalar quadratic bound, it serves as a complementary stage between cheap bounds and exact fallback.

[Heading2 ] 2.3 Six-scalar information model and certification hierarchy
[         ] The six retained summaries define an information map, not a physical channel model. Let ℐ𝒳 denote the six-scalar summary of a full configuration instance 𝒳=b,ĥ,C at a user, and let
[Equation ] 𝒞s=𝒳:ℐ𝒳=s(2c)
[         ] be the class of all instances indistinguishable to a certificate that observes only s. Throughout, the error radius r, the SINR level γ, the noise power σ2 and the dimensions n,m are fixed external parameters of the certification problem; they are not part of the summary s and are never varied inside an information class. A six-scalar certificate is any function Bs of the retained summaries that must remain valid across the entire class, Bs≤q*𝒳 for all 𝒳∈𝒞s, and the strongest possible uniform certificate under this information budget is therefore
[Equation ] B*s=inf𝒳∈𝒞s q*𝒳(2d)
[         ] Sections 3–5 characterize this quantity from the retained spectral information: Section 3 solves the directional extremum forced by the operator origin of the summaries; Section 4 proves that the resulting envelope equals B*s on the nondegenerate class and characterizes the separate optimum of the boundary class β0=α.
[         ] The information map defines a branched family of certification interfaces rather than a single linear ladder. With the same six scalars one may use the three gains separately (the separate-gain comparison envelope of this paper) or jointly through spectral compatibility (the envelope of this paper). Two distinct one-scalar augmentations are of interest: adjoining κ=∥A ĥ∥, the norm of the gradient of the quadratic objective, yields the quadratic-form bound Lquad=cq−2rκ−γβ02r2 of Section 7; adjoining H=∥ĥ∥ restricts the class itself, for which we give a necessary compatibility condition but no complete envelope. Finally, the full quadratic data support cheap bounds and exact trust-region or S-procedure solvers. The two augmentations are different information branches and are not merged into a single seven-scalar class. Adding information can only leave the strongest uniform certificate B* unchanged or tighter; it does not imply that any particular computable bound tightens, and indeed QG and Lquad exhibit no universal dominance relation in either direction. The interfaces also differ in cost structure: the six scalars and κ are built once from matrix geometry; their storage and transfer are then fixed-size; subsequent bound evaluation is scalar work; exact fallback, in contrast, requires the full quadratic data.
[         ] Why not retain one more scalar? Nothing prevents it: this paper answers the optimality question for exactly one fixed interface, and richer summaries — κ, H, or others — are legitimate alternatives whose complete envelopes are open. The six-scalar interface is studied because it is the smallest summary set that already exhibits nontrivial spectral compatibility, and because the answer is closed-form. We make no claim that six scalars are the best compression, that only this interface can be fixed-size, or any measured storage or communication saving.
[         ] Such a fixed-size interface can arise when candidate configurations are screened repeatedly, when configuration descriptors are cached or exchanged across network components, or when one wishes to evaluate a stored candidate at multiple uncertainty radii without retaining the complete quadratic geometry. These scenarios motivate the abstraction but are not assumptions required by the theory.
[Heading2 ] 2.4 Physical interpretation of the retained summaries
[         ] The retained quantities separate the desired direction from its orthogonal complement and summarize how one interference operator acts on these two subspaces. In wireless terms: p is the desired-stream transmit power; a is the nominal desired-signal amplitude along the desired beam; I0 is the nominal interference amplitude, whose square contributes to the interference power. The three spectral quantities describe how the interference operator maps channel error to interference: α is its response to error parallel to the desired beam; β is the largest amplification any error component orthogonal to the beam can suffer; β0 is the largest amplification any error at all can suffer.
[         ] The three spectral quantities should not be viewed as independent uncertainty parameters. They are coupled observations of the same interference operator. Spectral compatibility is therefore a structural consistency condition: a large response in one direction restricts what the same operator can realize in orthogonal directions.
[         ] A bound that uses these gains separately may combine individually valid worst cases that cannot be realized simultaneously by one operator and one shared error vector. The proposed directional extremum removes exactly this incompatibility while retaining the same six scalar summaries. Its improvement over the separate-gain envelope therefore comes from using more of the structure already contained in the retained information, rather than from introducing additional channel information.
[Heading2 ] 2.5 Related work
[         ] Robust transmission under imperfect CSI splits along the information available. Full-geometry worst-case design under norm-bounded errors is classical: S-procedure and semidefinite reformulations deliver exact or conservative constraints for MISO downlink, relay, secure, and RIS-aided systems, with [1] an early IRS-aided framework, [4] a current multi-functional RIS design under bounded cascaded CSI error, and [9] a representative worst-case SINR treatment that decomposes the steering-vector error into parallel and orthogonal components. Exact solution of the ball-constrained quadratic rests on the trust-region and S-lemma literature [2],[3]. Decomposing an error into parallel and orthogonal components and bounding the two effects separately is a recurring device in this line; the comparison envelope of the present paper — the shared-budget bound minβ0r,αx+βy assembled from the two standard norm constraints — is our baseline in that spirit; we have not found this exact envelope stated as a method in the literature and do not attribute it to any specific paper. A second line compresses information probabilistically, using distributional summaries with conservative approximations. Our question differs from both: the uncertainty is deterministic and bounded, and the compressed object is a fixed set of spectral summaries of one operator.

[         ] The mathematics we need is classical but dispersed, and we use it at the formula level detailed in Appendix A. To the best of our knowledge, within the searches reported in Appendix A, we have not found an existing result that evaluates the directional extremum in closed form under exactly the three retained spectral summaries and a shared error budget, nor an existing treatment of the β_0=α boundary's phase-coupled envelope; we do not claim these ingredients individually as new.

[Heading2 ] 6.1 Interval lower bounds
[         ] A sampled minimum is an upper estimate of a continuous minimum and must not be used for acceptance. We set x=r sinθ, y=r cosθ and partition 0,π/2 into Nθ cells. For a cell midpoint θm and width Δθ, use
[Equation ] RU=minβ0r, Rr sinθm,r cosθm+rα+βΔθ/2(29)
[         ] The Lipschitz bound |ΔR|≤α|Δx|+β|Δy| follows by representing the direction envelope as the supremum of the compatible linear output norms; the completion in Theorem 2 approaches the scalar extremum for each direction. Degenerate spectra have explicit compatible diagonal or collinear constructions (Lemma 1). Each fixed norm is Lipschitz with these constants, so their supremum is as well.
[         ] For the signal, use p times the squared distance from a to r sinθlo,r sinθhi. Subtract γI0+RU2+γσ2 and take the minimum cell lower bound. This produces QG,LB≤QG in real arithmetic. The separate-gain envelope uses the same partition and cap. Main comparisons use Nθ=256; refinements to 1024 and 4096 are reported separately. The analytical inequality is exact; the reported implementation uses floating-point arithmetic with conservative numerical safeguards rather than formally verified directed rounding.
[Heading2 ] 6.2 Engineering threshold and reference solver
[         ] For reference evaluation define A=bbH−γCCH, g=Aĥ and cq=ĥHAĥ−γσ2=pa2−γI02−γσ2. The margin is cq+eHAe+2ReeHg. The common engineering threshold is
[Equation ] τ=10−8max1,|cq|,2r∥g∥,r2∥A∥2(30)
[         ] The theoretical envelope depends only on six summaries; this experimental threshold and the reference validity checks additionally use full quadratic data. A method directly accepts a candidate only if every user's implemented lower bound exceeds its threshold. Otherwise it returns unknown. Reference statuses are clearly feasible for q*>τ, clearly infeasible for q*<−τ, ambiguous near zero, and invalid if numerical validity checks fail. Invalid references remain explicitly counted and cannot establish a rejection or a safety conclusion.
[         ] The reference solves the Hermitian trust region problem by eigenvalue decomposition and a multiplier search, handling PSD interior solutions, singular cases and the indefinite hard case. The global optimality conditions are stationarity, ball feasibility, A+μI≽0, μ≥0 and complementary slackness [2],[3]. Numerical checks inspect the full stationarity residual, shifted minimum eigenvalue, ball violation and complementarity. A 106 case audit reports a maximum primal–dual gap of 4.97×10−14. The fixed 200 iteration multiplier bisection used in early timing over resolves the multiplier: stopping the bisection once the bracket width falls below 10−12 of its magnitude changes no computed value by more than 5.7×10−10, preserves every validity check, and uses a median of 40 iterations. A 10−10 stop already shifts some values by up to 2.8×10−8 and is not used. All pipeline timings in Section 7 use the tolerance-stopped reference; a supplementary table reproduces the original 200 iteration protocol for transparency.
[         ] Fallback skips users already certified by the lower bound and stops at the first clearly infeasible user. Each user's eigenvalue factorization is reused across radii. Screening and reference calculations do not read the truth pass cache during timing. These implementation choices affect measured runtime and are not theoretical contributions.
[Heading2 ] 6.3 S procedure SDP baseline
[         ] As a full information baseline independent of our eigendecomposition reference, we adapt the standard single sphere S procedure LMI to the fixed candidate certification problem: for each user, maximize t subject to λ≥0 and
[Equation ] A+λIggHcq−t−λr2≽0,(31)
[         ] whose optimum equals q*r by the S lemma; the LMI is solved on the real embedding of the Hermitian data with an interior point solver [2],[3],[4]. This is a classical technique, representative of its use in robust RIS design [4], adapted to our certification interface; it is not a reproduction of that literature's system level algorithms and is not claimed as a new method.
[         ] The SDP provides a lower bound, but a negative lower bound proves nothing about infeasibility, and a solver status of optimal is not a certificate. Our decision rule is two sided. The lower bound L is the residual corrected dual feasible value: the shift t,λ→t−η1+r2,λ+η adds blockdiagηI,η to the LMI, so Weyl's inequality restores positive semidefiniteness up to eigensolver rounding, with an empirical rounding allowance and a numerical recheck; this is an implementation detail, not a formal error bound. Infeasibility is certified only by an upper bound: from the corrected multiplier we recover the Lagrangian minimizer A+λ'Ie=−g, project it onto the ball if ∥e∥>r, and re evaluate the true objective U=qew. Since ew is ball feasible, U is a valid upper bound of q*, and U<−τ certifies infeasibility; no optimality of ew is claimed. All remaining cases return unresolved and are never treated as rejections.

[Heading1 ] 8 Interface to configuration lifecycle management
[         ] The certificate addresses whether a fixed candidate can be deployed at a prescribed uncertainty radius. Once a candidate is selected, a separate complete configuration reference can determine its robust radius for subsequent online retain or refresh management. Candidate generation, deployment costs and channel drift remain outside the present problem. A positive certificate at one radius is not a maximum radius certificate.
[Heading1 ] 9 Discussion
[         ] The sharpness statement is intentionally strong and narrow: it holds for the unrestricted six-scalar information class at fixed r,γ,σ2 and dimensions, whose extremal constructions may require large nominal channel norms that a physical RIS channel may not admit. Restricting the class — for example by adjoining H=∥ĥ∥, for which (28) gives a necessary compatibility condition — can only shrink the admissible set, and the six-scalar envelope need not remain sharp; the complete envelopes of the H-branch and of other richer interfaces are open. Empirically, the certificate gain over the separate-gain envelope is positive in the sensitivity, validation and heterogeneous-link batches and zero in the saturated easy batch; no stable runtime advantage over the separate-gain pipeline is observed, and measured speedups are tied to the tested implementation. The certificate is complementary to, not a replacement for, full-information methods: within the branched family of interfaces it is the strongest guarantee available at its information level, and combining interfaces by maximization is safe.
[Heading1 ] 10 Conclusion
[         ] Mathematically, joint spectral compatibility of three gains of one interference operator yields an explicit three-branch directional extremum, a safe shared-budget QoS envelope dominating the separate-gain envelope, equality with the information-class infimum away from the degenerate boundary together with an explicit approximating construction, and a separately derived, explicitly attained phase-coupled envelope at that boundary. Practically, the envelope is a fixed-size certificate whose empirical gain over the separate-gain envelope is batch-dependent and modest, and which is complementary to a quadratic bound retaining one additional scalar — the two have no universal dominance relation and combine safely by maximization, giving the highest direct-certification coverage among the tested bounds. In scope, the certificate does not replace exact full-information solvers, does not optimize candidate generation, and its sharpness concerns the stated unrestricted information class; natural extensions include richer retained summaries such as the H-branch, physically restricted information classes, validated numerical certificates, and lifecycle management of stored configuration certificates.
